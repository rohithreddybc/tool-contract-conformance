## Interpretation, recorded by the session owner 2026-08-29

**The flip is about demonstrability, not truth, and the paper must say so precisely.**

`reserve_car_rental`'s `arg.end_time` resolves VIOLATES/ignored_argument under Annotator A's contract, with three successful probe variants and the result-disagrees-with-state signal, and UNTESTABLE/`no_observable_state` under Annotator B's, with **zero** successful variants. Read carelessly this says a finding is annotator-dependent. Read correctly it says something narrower and more useful.

Both annotators observed the defect independently. B, working blind, recorded in its own notes that line 399 assigns `reservation.end_time` from `start_time` rather than from the `end_time` argument, and wrote the effect clause from the documented promise anyway — which is what the protocol asks for. The four effect-clause comparisons on this tool agree at 100%, including the Finding 6 clause itself.

What differs is whether the contract lets the checker *reach* the code. Under B's contract no probe variant produced a successful call, so the Ignored Argument prober had nothing to compare and correctly returned UNTESTABLE rather than a false negative. Nine of the eleven corpus probes failed the call outright under both contracts.

So the finding's **existence** is robust across annotators; its **mechanical demonstration** is not. Contract details — which preconditions are declared, whether `probe_values` are supplied — determine whether the prober can construct a call that exercises the defect at all.

**This is the same root cause as the tau2 open-world arm's zero denominator and, pending R7, likely a share of the closed-world misses**: the pre-registered recorded-real-calls corpus component was never built, so probe generation falls back on type-generic values that fail existence checks on entity-keyed tools. Three separate results in this paper now trace to one missing artifact. That is the single highest-value piece of future work and §X should say so in those terms.

**How §V must report this.** Not "no finding flipped" — one did. Not "findings are annotator-dependent" — the defect was seen by both. The accurate sentence is that a finding's demonstrability depends on contract authoring in a way its existence does not, and the mechanism is probe reachability rather than disagreement about what the interface advertises.

**Two limits on this study, both load-bearing:**
- `cancel_reservation` is contaminated and excluded from the blind stratum. `PREDICATE-GRAMMAR.md` §5, which the protocol requires B to read, is a complete worked example of that exact tool. The protocol as written cannot produce a blind annotation for it — a defect in our protocol, not in B's work.
- `venmo_social`'s `arg.sort_by` anchor is **incomparable**: B did not declare that argument in its signature at all, so the finding could not be tested under B's contract. Reported separately and never counted as agreement.
