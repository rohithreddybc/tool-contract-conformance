## Interpretation — appended by the main session, 2026-08-29

*Provenance note: this section is hand-written by the session owner, not emitted by `experiments/agreement.py`. An earlier copy was appended and then removed when the script regenerated the file; the agent that regenerated it correctly flagged the unexplained write rather than absorbing it, which is the right instinct for an artifact that is supposed to be generated-only. Anything below this line is commentary, not generated output.*

**The blind stratum agrees completely where calls succeed: 98 of 98 probe comparisons, 16 mapped clause pairs, zero predicate-level disagreements.** Translating an advertised sentence into an executable predicate was stable across two annotators who did not share knowledge of which tools were defective.

**One finding flips, and the cause is an annotation gap, not a disagreement.** `reserve_car_rental`'s `arg.end_time` is VIOLATES under A and UNTESTABLE under B. A declares `probe_values` pinning `start_time` and `end_time` to parseable ISO strings; B does not. Every Ignored Argument variant the prober builds from B's contract therefore dies inside `datetime.fromisoformat()` on a harvested non-date string — `'Paris'` in one case — before reaching the tool's effect code. Zero of three variants produce a successful call, so the verdict is UNTESTABLE rather than a false CONFORMS.

Both annotators observed the defect independently; B's blind notes record that line 399 assigns `end_time` from `start_time`. All four effect-clause comparisons on this tool, including the Finding 6 clause, agree at 100% wherever a real call succeeds.

So the accurate sentence for §V is: **a finding's demonstrability depends on whether the contract supplies enough annotation for the prober to construct a reaching call; its existence does not.** Not "no finding flipped", which is false. Not "findings are annotator-dependent", which misdescribes the mechanism.

**One anchor is incomparable rather than agreeing.** `venmo_social`'s `arg.sort_by` could not be tested under B's contract at all — B never declared that argument. A and B operationalized entirely disjoint dispatch branches of the same multiplexed tool: A took `comment/list`, B took `friend add/remove` and `like toggle`, with zero shared clauses and zero shared argument names. That is a segmentation result about multiplexed tools, and it is reported as incomparable, never as agreement.

**The protocol could not be executed as written, in a way that matters.** Three tools — `refuel_data`, `cancel_reservation`, `update_scheduled_transaction` — required hand-built anchor-witness probes that read real entity ids off a live snapshot. The frozen §2 corpus generator has no mechanism to harvest a real database key, so every boundary and precondition probe it produces fails these tools' existence checks. Without the added probes, every effect, invariant and frame clause on those three tools would have been graded exclusively on failed no-op calls, yielding a spurious 100% agreement that measured nothing. The addition was made and disclosed before any outcome was inspected.

**This is the same missing artifact as everywhere else.** The pre-registered recorded-real-calls corpus component (`detector_analysis_plan.md` §2, item 1) does not exist anywhere in the project; every production caller already passes an empty list. It is now implicated in the tau2 open-world zero denominator, this study's need for hand-built witnesses, and — pending the R7 decomposition — a share of the closed-world misses. §X should name it as the single highest-value piece of future work, in those terms.

**One thing to check before submission**, surfaced by B and not yet adjudicated: B's `frame.other_users_unchanged` on `invite_user_to_slack` VIOLATES on every probe. B's own notes flag it as a debatable path-scoping choice — an "unchanged" claim over a wildcard that includes a growing list. It is either a real unadvertised-side-effect signal or an over-broad frame path, and it is currently neither confirmed nor dismissed.
