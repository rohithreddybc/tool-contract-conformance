# Detector Validation — Pre-Registered Analysis Plan

**Status: to be committed at tag `checker-freeze-v1`, before any mutant is generated or scored.** The commit hash is cited in the paper's §VII. Nothing in this document may be revised after the first mutant is scored; if it must be, see §8.

This artifact exists because the earlier plan committed only the *agent experiment's* analysis, on Sep 5, while the mutation arms were scored Sep 2-4. That left every mutation-arm statistical choice — interval method, clustering, escape-rate denominator, equivalence adjudication — formally free to be chosen after the data were seen. Repairs W4 and W5 of the methodology review.

---

## 1. Two arms, and what each licenses

| Arm | Question | What a good result licenses | What it does not license |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closed-world | Given a defect of a class we defined, does the checker detect it? | "The checker detects violations of clauses we wrote" | Any claim about coverage of the defect space |
| Open-world | Given a defect nobody designed for our taxonomy, does the checker notice? | A **lower bound** on incompleteness | Completeness. Ever. |

The open-world escape rate is reported whatever it says. A high escape rate is a result about the taxonomy, published as such; §7 fixes how it is described so that framing cannot be chosen later.

## 2. The equivalence probe corpus

Fixed and committed at `checker-freeze-v1`, before any mutant exists. Distinct from the checker's own probes — see §4.3, where the distinction does load-bearing work.

For each tool in the corpus, the probe set is:
1. Every recorded real call captured during adapter smoke-testing.
2. Boundary probes generated by a committed script from the tool's signature: for each parameter, a valid value, a type-valid but domain-invalid value, and where the type admits it, a zero/empty value.
3. For each precondition clause in the tool's contract, one probe satisfying it and one violating it.

The corpus is frozen with the checker. Probes are never added to justify a result.

## 3. Closed-world arm

**Operators.** Six, one per defect class, as specified in `ARCHITECTURE-FINAL.md` §5. AST-level, generic, no per-benchmark tuning.

**Sites.** Enumerated programmatically by `mutation/sites.py` across all conformant tools. The tools carrying confirmed findings are **excluded from the mutation corpus** — they are the anchor cases, and mutating them would let the checker rediscover what it already found.

**Sample.** ~25 mutants per class, drawn by seeded random selection from the enumerated site pool. Seed committed here. Where a class has fewer than 25 available sites, the actual number is reported and no substitution from another class is made.

**Precision controls.** ~30 semantics-preserving mutants that must NOT be flagged. Per W5 of the review, at least half must be refactorings of *advertised* behavior — reorder independent writes, extract a guard into a helper, arithmetically equivalent expressions — not log-string or variable-name edits, which are undetectable by construction and prove nothing.

**Reported.** Precision = true detections / all flags. Recall = detected / injected non-equivalent. Per class, per tool, with intervals per §5.

## 4. Open-world arm

**Tool.** cosmic-ray 8.7.0, chosen empirically: mutmut 3.7.0 refuses to run on Windows and WSL is not installed. Operators are cosmic-ray's own and are blind to our taxonomy, which is the entire point. Config requires absolute paths in `test-command` — relative paths silently yield all-`INCOMPETENT` results.

### 4.1 Survival, defined mechanically

cosmic-ray's own vocabulary assumes a test suite decides life and death. There is no test suite here, so survival is redefined and the redefinition is committed:

> A mutant is **behaviourally live** iff, on at least one probe in the §2 corpus, it produces a canonicalized post-state or a canonicalized result differing from the unmutated tool.

Behaviourally dead mutants are the **mechanical equivalence screen**. They leave the denominator with no human judgment involved, which is the point: W5 identified author-adjudicated equivalence, applied after seeing which mutants were missed, as the largest single degree of freedom in the design.

### 4.2 Escape rate

> **Escape** = a behaviourally live mutant that the conformance checker does not report as VIOLATES.
> **Escape rate** = escapes / behaviourally live mutants.

Both a **raw** and an **adjudicated** rate are reported. The adjudicated rate permits reclassifying a live mutant as semantically equivalent only under the written rules in §4.4, and the gap between raw and adjudicated is itself reported — a large gap means the rules did a lot of work and the reader is entitled to see that.

### 4.3 Every escape is classified into exactly one of three causes

Reported as a decomposition, never as a single number:

| Cause | Meaning | What it implies |
|---|---|---|
| **Clause gap** | No contract clause covers the behaviour the mutant changed | A genuine taxonomy or contract-depth limitation. The interesting one |
| **Probe gap** | A clause covers it, but the checker's own generated probes never drove the tool into the state that exposes it | A limitation of `dynamic/probes.py`, not of the contract format |
| **Canonicalization gap** | The difference was masked as a declared-volatile field | A limitation of `core/canonical.py`, and a precision/recall trade we made deliberately |

This decomposition is why §2's corpus and the checker's own probes must stay distinct: if the same probes both screened equivalence and drove the checker, probe gaps would be invisible by construction.

Classification is mechanical where possible — clause gap is decidable by checking whether any clause's predicate reads the changed state path; canonicalization gap by re-running with volatility masking disabled. Only genuinely ambiguous cases go to §4.4.

### 4.4 Equivalence adjudication rules — committed before scoring

A behaviourally live mutant may be reclassified as equivalent only if one of these holds, and the applicable rule is recorded per mutant:

1. The changed behaviour is invisible at the tool's interface — it alters an internal value that no return, no state field, and no error can reflect.
2. The change affects only a field declared volatile in the canonicalization rules committed at freeze time.
3. The change is a semantics-preserving refactoring under the same definition used for the §3 precision controls.

Not admissible: "the checker's failure to catch it seems reasonable"; "this defect class is out of scope"; "no real benchmark would have this bug."

A sample of at least 20% of adjudication calls is dual-adjudicated by the second annotator, and disagreement is reported.

## 5. Statistics

- **Intervals.** Wilson score, 95%. Every rate is reported with its interval. **Claims are stated at the interval's lower bound, never at the point estimate** — 20/25 is a point estimate of 0.80 and a Wilson interval of [0.61, 0.91], and "recall ≥ 0.8" would be compatible with a true recall of 0.62.
- **Clustering.** Mutants within one tool share a contract, an author, and a code style; they are not independent trials. Per-tool breakdowns are reported alongside every pooled rate, and pooled intervals are computed with a tool-clustered method.

  **The specific method, named here because "a tool-clustered method" was underspecified.** Pooled intervals use a **design-effect-adjusted Wilson interval**: the Kish design effect with an ICC estimated by the Fleiss/Rao-Scott one-way ANOVA estimator, applied to the effective sample size before the Wilson bounds are computed. Closed form, deterministic, no resampling, and no hypothesis test — which matters, because a bootstrap over clustered units is precisely the error behind a prior rejection.

  *Amendment recorded: this paragraph was made concrete on 2026-08-21, **before `checker-freeze-v1` was tagged and before any mutant was generated or scored.** The implementation flagged the underspecification rather than silently choosing a method, which is the behaviour this document exists to enforce. Specifying an estimator after seeing data would have been the violation; specifying it before is the plan working.*
- **No hypothesis tests.** No p-values, no significance language, in this arm. There is no comparison for which a test is the right instrument.
- **Denominators are printed with every rate.** UNTESTABLE clauses stay in all denominators with their reason codes, per `ARCHITECTURE-FINAL.md` §3.5.

## 6. Contract coverage, reported alongside

Per tool: the fraction of advertised-surface sentences operationalized into clauses, and the fraction of state-writing statements covered by some effect or frame clause. High recall on shallow contracts is not evidence of anything, and these two numbers must be read together. Also reported: `biconditional: true` adoption (n of the in-scope tools, with justifications), since Phantom Effect's detection surface is bounded by it.

## 7. Committed framing

Fixed now so it cannot be chosen after seeing the numbers:

- The escape rate is described as **a lower bound on the taxonomy's incompleteness**, measured with operators that know nothing about it. It is never described as evidence of coverage or completeness.
- If the escape rate is high, the paper reports it, decomposes it per §4.3, and — if a seventh defect class is evident from the clause-gap escapes — names it as **future work**. The taxonomy is not retrofitted after freeze.
- Closed-world recall is described as "detects violations of the clauses we authored," never as "detects tool-contract defects."

## 8. Refreeze

If the checker changes after `checker-freeze-v1`, the mutant sample is invalidated. Scoring reruns on **freshly drawn sites from the unused pool**, under a new tag, and the paper reports the number of refreeze cycles. The tags and the generation script ship in the artifact so a reviewer can verify the ordering from git history rather than taking our word for it.

## 9. Deliberately not done

- No comparison against another detector. There is no baseline that tests this contract layer; inventing a weak one to beat would be theatre.
- No cross-benchmark pooling of rates.
- No claim that the six classes are exhaustive.
- No per-benchmark operator tuning, and no reporting of cross-*benchmark* transfer for the closed-world arm — MedAgentBench contributes one no-op code path and cannot support five of the six operators. Cross-*domain* transfer within tau2 is reported instead, and is real.
